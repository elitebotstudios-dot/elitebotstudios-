Smart Energy Monitoring

A project direction exploring how embedded measurement might make electrical energy data easier to understand.

ENGINEERING OBJECTIVE

Explore how embedded measurement and electrical energy data can be combined in a documented monitoring system.

Concept

This page describes a concept and its open questions. It is not a fabricated device, schematic, simulation, or test report.

OVERVIEW

What question is this exploring?

A design brief for an energy-monitoring system, written before any hardware exists. It fixes the problem, the requirements and the open engineering choices, so the eventual schematic and firmware can be judged against something rather than invented after the fact.

PROJECT NOTE

Questions to resolve

  • Which measurable quantity and operating range should the first study address?
  • Shunt, Hall-effect, or integrated monitor — which trade-off is worth learning first?
  • How is the ADC reference stabilised so the measurement means something?
  • What information would make the output useful without implying more accuracy than the measurement supports?

Possible next investigations

  • Publish the requirements table once the operating range is chosen.

INTENDED APPLICATION

Where it would be used.

The intended application is low-voltage, supervised measurement of an appliance or small installation: enough to answer “what is this load actually drawing, and when?” without claiming revenue-grade accuracy or mains-connected deployment.

REQUIREMENTS / PROPOSED

What the design has to satisfy.

Proposed requirements
R1Measure voltage and current of a single low-voltage DC load at a useful rate (at least tens of samples per second) and derive power.
R2Keep measurement uncertainty stated, with a calibration procedure against a reference meter.
R3Record or display data locally so a network outage does not lose the measurement.
R4Stay inside a supervised, low-voltage boundary; any mains-referenced variant requires isolation review before it is attempted.
R5Publish the architecture, calibration method and test conditions alongside any number, so a reader can check the claim.

Requirements are proposals for review, not accepted specifications.

SENSING OPTIONS / PROPOSED

Possible sensing methods.

Nothing is selected yet. Each method is listed with the trade-off that would decide it.

Possible sensing methods.
Shunt resistor + current-sense amplifierBest linearity and low-side simplicity; adds a small series drop and needs attention to common-mode range if used high-side.
Hall-effect current sensorIsolated by construction, simpler to wire, but offset drift and temperature behaviour need checking against the datasheet.
Resistive divider for voltageCheap and predictable, but the divider loads the node and its impedance must suit the ADC sample-and-hold.
Energy-monitoring IC with integrated ADCsFewer analog decisions to make, less of the signal chain to learn — a trade of effort against understanding.
CONTROLLER OPTIONS / PROPOSED

Controller candidates.

Controller choice follows the measurement decisions, not the other way round.

Controller candidates.
ESP32 classWi-Fi and a capable ADC on one part; ADC linearity needs care and calibration.
STM32 classBetter analog peripherals and deterministic timing; connectivity needs additional design.
External ADC + any MCUMoves accuracy requirements into a component with a proper datasheet, at the cost of board complexity.
SAFETY & ISOLATION

Where this study stops.

The first study stays low-voltage and supervised. A mains-referenced version would need galvanic isolation, creepage and clearance decisions, protective devices and review by someone qualified to sign it off — that is out of scope for this record.

VALIDATION STRATEGY

How a result would be checked.

  • Compare against a calibrated reference meter at several operating points and record the deviation.
  • State ambient temperature, instrument, wiring and load for every measurement.
  • Repeat measurements over time to separate drift from noise.
  • Publish failed checks as well as passing ones.
