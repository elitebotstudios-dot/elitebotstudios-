DC energy monitor.
The hardware layer.

A measurement carrier between a low-voltage bench supply and a load: fuse, reverse-polarity diode, transient clamp, a 0.1 Ω shunt and an INA226, read by an ESP32-DevKitC over I²C. Schematics, netlist, bill of materials, design review, firmware and calibration plan are published together.

Design drafted and script-reviewed KiCad ERC not run Firmware not compiled for the board Prototype not built
01 / WHERE THE PROJECT STANDS

Eight stages,
four of them done.

Each stage is reported at the level of evidence that actually exists. Nothing here is marked complete because it was written down.

  1. ConceptCompletedDesign brief and open questions, published 2026-11.
  2. Software modelCompleted and testedVerified against seven reference cases plus 594 independent checks. The instrument runs in the browser.
  3. Hardware requirementsCompleted (draft for review)E1–E12 written, each marked verified or proposed. No requirement is claimed as measured.
  4. Electrical designDraft — reviewed by script, not by ERCTwo schematic sheets generated from one netlist; 25 design-rule checks pass. No EDA tool was available, so KiCad ERC has not been run.
  5. Embedded firmwareDesktop tested — never compiled for the boardThe calculation core compiles and passes 69 checks with g++. The ESP32 application layer has never been built or flashed.
  6. Physical prototypeNot builtNo component has been purchased, assembled or powered.
  7. CalibrationNot startedProcedure and blank recording sheet prepared; no calibration has been performed.
  8. Physical validationNot availableNo measurement against a reference instrument exists.

Software and hardware status are deliberately separate. A verified simulation says nothing about a circuit that has never been powered.

02 / ARCHITECTURE

Source, protection,
shunt, sensor.

Four things happen between the supply and the load, and each one costs something that is written down.

01

Input protection

A 1.1 A hold resettable fuse, a 3 A 40 V Schottky for reverse polarity, and a 15 V transient clamp that conducts at 24.4 V — below the sensor's 36 V absolute maximum.

Cost: 0.3–0.4 V of headroom and about 0.2 W at 0.5 A through the diode.

02

Current shunt

0.1 Ω, 0.5 %, 1 W in a 2512 package, in series with the load. At 0.5 A it drops 50 mV and dissipates 25 mW — 2 % of its rating.

Cost: the burden voltage, and a hard measurable limit of 0.819 A.

03

Measurement

An INA226 measures the shunt drop and the load-side bus voltage, both 16-bit, with ±81.92 mV and 0–36 V ranges. It sits on the 3.3 V rail, so no level shifting is needed.

Cost: the module is the least-defined part — see the open questions.

04

Controller and link

An ESP32-DevKitC reads the sensor over I²C at 0x40 on GPIO21/22, with 4.7 kΩ pull-ups to 3.3 V on the carrier, accumulates energy and prints CSV on the USB serial port.

Cost: no real-time clock, so timestamps are milliseconds since boot.

03 / SCHEMATIC

Two sheets,
generated from one netlist.

Both sheets are drawn from design/dcm-hw-v1.json by a script that refuses to emit a drawing with a floating connection. Open either at full size, or regenerate and check the output yourself.

WHAT HAS NOT BEEN DONE

No KiCad project exists and no ERC has been run. No EDA tool was available in the environment where this was designed. Instead, the circuit is described in one JSON file; a generator draws the schematic and emits a KiCad netlist; and design/checks.py checks the 25 electrical rules this design depends on, printing the margin for each. That is not the same as ERC, and it is not presented as such. The next step for the owner is to redraw the sheets in KiCad, import or re-enter the netlist, and run ERC.

06 / FIRMWARE

A core that runs
on a desktop.

The arithmetic lives in a platform-free C++ core with no Arduino headers, so it can be compiled and tested without a board. The application layer that touches I²C and the serial port is separate.

Modules

  • Register decode (INA226, no I²C)
  • Calibration per channel
  • Power and trapezoidal energy
  • Missing-data policy and counters
  • I²C and serial application layer

Missing-data policy

An invalid reading costs two intervals — the one ending at it and the one starting from it. A gap longer than five seconds is not integrated at all. Both are counted, so a period with no measurement cannot look like a period of zero power.

Timing

Milliseconds since boot in an unsigned 32-bit counter; subtraction wraps correctly after 49.7 days. There is no real-time clock in this design, so no date or time of day is claimed.

What has run

The core compiles with g++ -std=c++17 -Wall -Wextra and passes 69 checks across eight vector sets. The ESP32 application layer has never been compiled, because no ESP32 toolchain was available.

07 / OPEN QUESTIONS

What only the
physical module can answer.

Six questions that must be answered with a multimeter before the ESP32 is connected to anything.

  1. Address strapsDoes the module actually sit at 0x40? Read it with an I²C scanner.
  2. Pull-up railDo the module's pull-ups go to 3.3 V, 5 V, or the module supply? Measure before wiring.
  3. VBUS connectionIs the bus voltage taken at IN−? If the pin is separate, wire it there.
  4. On-board shuntWhat value is fitted, and does it sit in parallel with the external 0.1 Ω?
  5. Common-mode rangeDoes the module's amplifier accept a 12 V common-mode at the shunt?
  6. Supply qualityDoes the 3.3 V rail hold up under I²C traffic?
