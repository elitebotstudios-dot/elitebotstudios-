01 / PROBLEM

Energy data is only useful if you know how far to trust it.

A design brief for a low-voltage DC energy-monitoring system, and the first working artefact that follows it: a software model of the whole measurement chain — DC source, load, voltage and current sensing, ADC quantisation, sampled power and trapezoidal energy accumulation — with the formulas, source, reference cases and an independent verification suite published alongside it.

A monitoring system that reports 12.00 W is making a claim about a measurement chain: a divider, a shunt, an amplifier, an ADC, an arithmetic rule and a clock. Each of those contributes an error, and the errors do not cancel. The useful question is not "what does it read?" but "how far can that reading be from the truth, and why?"

02 / OBJECTIVE

Build the measurement chain in software first.

The intended application is low-voltage, supervised measurement of an appliance or small installation: enough to answer “what is this load actually drawing, and when?” without claiming revenue-grade accuracy or mains-connected deployment.

V1 has one job: implement the whole chain so that every stage can be inspected, varied and tested independently. A student building the hardware next year should be able to point at the model and say "this is what the firmware must compute, these are the error terms it must tolerate, and this is the test it must pass".

  • In scope: DC source and load models, configurable sensing with gain, offset, noise and quantisation, sampled power, trapezoidal energy, CSV logging, an interactive instrument.
  • Out of scope: AC or mains measurement, isolation, PCB design, firmware, calibration against a reference meter, any accuracy claim about hardware.
03 / SYSTEM DESIGN

Two paths through the same signal.

The model solves the electrical operating point at every sample instant, then passes it through the sensing chain. That gives two numbers per channel — the truth and what a firmware would report — and the difference between them is the subject of the whole project.

04 / ENGINEERING MATHEMATICS

Power, and the integral that turns it into energy.

Instantaneous power
P=V·I

Computed twice: once from the reported voltage and current, once from the simulated truth.

Energy, discretely
Ej=Ej−1+Pj−1+Pj2·(tj−tj−1)

The interval comes from the timestamps, never from an assumed period.

Watt-hours
EWh=EJ3600

Accumulators hold joules; 1 Wh = 3600 J exactly.

The trapezoidal rule is second order: halving the sample interval quarters the integration error. The independent suite checks that convergence rate directly rather than trusting it.

05 / SOFTWARE ARCHITECTURE

One module, no dependencies, two consumers.

energy-monitor/model/dcem.js holds all of the physics and arithmetic. The dashboard is the second consumer of that file, not a reimplementation: if the model and the instrument could disagree, that would defeat the purpose.

  1. 01
    Source and load

    Solved together so voltage, current and resistance cannot contradict each other.

  2. 02
    Sensing and ADC

    Sensitivity, gain error, offset, noise, clamping and uniform quantisation, per channel.

  3. 03
    Sampling and validation

    Timestamps, rejection of duplicate or non-monotonic samples, counters instead of silence.

  4. 04
    Power and energy

    Trapezoidal accumulation on the real interval, with imported and exported energy separated.

  5. 05
    Logging

    CSV with both truth and measurement columns, written locally.

06 / VERIFICATION

Seven reference cases, two independent methods.

The reference suite asserts hand-derived values with stated tolerances. The independent suite compares the model against a different construction — a second integrator over 500 random series, a closed-form sine integral with a convergence-order check, Ohm's law and Kirchhoff balance, additivity across consecutive runs, and statistics on the noise generator.

07 / TRADE-OFFS

Choices that cost something.

Trapezoidal instead of rectangular
Second-order accuracy for two multiplications and one extra stored sample. The rectangular rule would have been simpler and would have biased every step.
Per-channel calibration
Two independent corrections instead of one shared one. A divider and a shunt amplifier do not share a tolerance, and an early version of the model got this wrong.
Ideal quantisation only
The model does not reproduce INL, DNL or reference drift. They matter for a real ADC and would need datasheet numbers to model honestly.
Seeded noise instead of random
Reproducible runs and testable statistics, at the cost of not being able to show a "different" run on each reload.
No filtering by default
A first-order low-pass is available but off, because it changes reference results. Turning it on is a decision the user makes, not a hidden default.
Browser-side only
No server, no account, no upload. The cost is that a run lives and dies in the tab unless it is exported.
08 / RESULTS

What the software demonstrates.

  • A DC power and energy calculation that matches hand-derived values exactly where the mathematics is exact, and to a quantified tolerance where it is not.
  • A sensing chain whose gain, offset, noise and quantisation each produce the error the equations predict, with calibration recovering the value.
  • A sampling rule that uses real timestamps, so irregular intervals integrate correctly.
  • Honest failure: invalid data is refused and counted rather than integrated into a plausible-looking total.
  • An instrument that shows the truth and the measurement at the same time, so the difference is never hidden behind a single number.
09 / LIMITATIONS

What it does not establish.

  • No hardware accuracy of any kind: no sensor, ADC, MCU or board has been used.
  • No AC, mains, isolation or grid behaviour.
  • No switched-mode or fast-transient load behaviour; loads that change faster than the sample interval are aliased.
  • No ADC non-idealities, no fixed-point arithmetic, no timer jitter.
  • No filtering design and no loop response.

The full list is in limitations.md.

Open questions

  • Which measurable quantity and operating range should the first study address?
  • Shunt, Hall-effect, or integrated monitor — which trade-off is worth learning first?
  • How is the ADC reference stabilised so the measurement means something?
  • What information would make the output useful without implying more accuracy than the measurement supports?

Sensing methods to compare

Sensing options
Shunt resistor + current-sense amplifierBest linearity and low-side simplicity; adds a small series drop and needs attention to common-mode range if used high-side.
Hall-effect current sensorIsolated by construction, simpler to wire, but offset drift and temperature behaviour need checking against the datasheet.
Resistive divider for voltageCheap and predictable, but the divider loads the node and its impedance must suit the ADC sample-and-hold.
Energy-monitoring IC with integrated ADCsFewer analog decisions to make, less of the signal chain to learn — a trade of effort against understanding.

Nothing is selected. Each method is listed with the trade-off that would decide it.

Controller candidates

Controller options
ESP32 classWi-Fi and a capable ADC on one part; ADC linearity needs care and calibration.

External ADC + any MCUMoves accuracy requirements into a component with a proper datasheet, at the cost of board complexity.

Controller choice follows the measurement decisions, not the other way round.

SAFETY AND ISOLATION

The first study stays low-voltage and supervised. A mains-referenced version would need galvanic isolation, creepage and clearance decisions, protective devices and review by someone qualified to sign it off — that is out of scope for this record.

The first build is limited to a supervised bench supply at 24 V DC or less with a current limit of 1 A or less, an inline fuse, and a second person present. No mains wiring.

HOW A HARDWARE RESULT WOULD BE CHECKED
  • Run the reference suite and compare its printed table with the published values before trusting any number on the site.
  • Re-derive the power and energy relations independently; the independent suite exists so a self-consistent error cannot pass both.
  • For hardware, compare against a calibrated reference meter at several operating points and record the instrument, wiring, load and ambient conditions.
  • Publish failed checks as well as passing ones, and state the tolerance with every claim.
