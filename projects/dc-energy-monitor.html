01 / EXPERIMENTS

Five things
worth looking at.

Each experiment loads a complete configuration into the instrument above. They all use the same validated model — there is no second implementation for the demonstrations.

EXPERIMENT 01

Constant DC load

Voltage, current, power and energy in the simplest case.

A 12 V supply into a 12 Ω resistive load. The current follows from the supply and the resistance, and the energy graph is a straight line whose slope is the power.

EXPERIMENT 02

Changing load

What a step does to power and to the energy slope.

The load current halves part-way through the run. Power halves with it, so the energy trace bends rather than stopping.

EXPERIMENT 03

ADC quantisation

Ideal signal against a quantised measurement.

An 8-bit converter on a slowly varying signal. The measured trace is a staircase; every step is one LSB of the sensing chain.

EXPERIMENT 04

Calibration and error

Systematic error, random error, and the correction.

Gain error and offset bias the reading in a predictable direction. Calibration removes the systematic part; the noise remains.

EXPERIMENT 05

Sampling interval

What sparse samples cannot tell you.

A 4 s duty cycle sampled every 2 s. The reported average depends on where the samples land, which is a property of the sampling, not of the load.

02 / THE MODEL

Power, energy,
and the time between samples.

Discrete samples do not measure instantaneous power; they bracket it. The model says exactly what it does between two samples.

Power

P=V·I

Computed from the two measured channels after scaling and quantisation, and separately from the simulated truth so the difference can be read directly.

Energy, trapezoidal

Ej+=Pj−1+Pj2·Δtj

Each interval contributes the average of its two endpoint powers times the actual elapsed time. Power is never summed without multiplying by time.

Units

EWh=EJ3600

Accumulators hold joules; the interface shows watt-hours. 1 Wh = 3600 J exactly, with no rounding in between.

Sensing chain

Vpin=V·S·(1+g)+Vos+n

Sensitivity, gain error, offset and noise at the pin, then uniform quantisation to the nearest LSB. A channel with 0 bits is treated as ideal.

What the numbers between samples mean

  • The simulated truth is evaluated at each sample instant. Between samples it is undefined — the model does not interpolate a "true" waveform.
  • The trapezoidal rule assumes power varies linearly across an interval. That is exact for constant power, slightly high or low across a step, and approximate for anything curved.
  • A step that falls between two samples is smeared across that interval. Reference case C quantifies it: 2.5 J in 27 000 J, or 0.0093 %.
  • Imported and exported energy are integrated separately, splitting the interval at a sign change, so reversed current cannot reduce a consumption total by accident.
  • Rejected samples — duplicate, non-monotonic, non-numeric or out of range — are counted and never integrated.
