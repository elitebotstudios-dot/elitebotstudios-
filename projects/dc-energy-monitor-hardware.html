DC energy monitor.
The hardware layer.

A measurement carrier between a low-voltage bench supply and a load: fuse, reverse-polarity diode, transient clamp, a 0.1 Ω shunt and an INA226 wired as the bare IC, read by an ESP32-DevKitC over I²C. The schematic is a native KiCad project that passes ERC with no errors and no warnings, and the firmware compiles for the board it targets.

02 / ARCHITECTURE

Source, protection,
shunt, sensor.

Four things happen between the supply and the load, and each one costs something that is written down.

01

Input protection

A 1.1 A hold resettable fuse, a 3 A 40 V Schottky for reverse polarity, and a 15 V transient clamp that conducts at 24.4 V — below the sensor's 36 V absolute maximum.

Cost: 0.3–0.4 V of headroom and about 0.2 W at 0.5 A through the diode.

02

Current shunt

0.1 Ω, 0.5 %, 1 W in a 2512 package, in series with the load. At 0.5 A it drops 50 mV and dissipates 25 mW — 2 % of its rating.

Cost: the burden voltage, and a hard measurable limit of 0.819 A.

03

Measurement

An INA226 measures the shunt drop and the load-side bus voltage, both 16-bit, with ±81.92 mV and 0–36 V ranges. It sits on the 3.3 V rail, so no level shifting is needed.

Cost: the module is the least-defined part — see the open questions.

04

Controller and link

An ESP32-DevKitC reads the sensor over I²C at 0x40 on GPIO21/22, with 4.7 kΩ pull-ups to 3.3 V on the carrier, accumulates energy and prints CSV on the USB serial port.

Cost: no real-time clock, so timestamps are milliseconds since boot.

What ERC checked

Pin conflicts, unconnected inputs, power-input pins without a driver, output-to-output clashes, and library consistency. The first run produced two real errors — two power outputs on one net — which were fixed by correcting the symbol pin types rather than by disabling the rule.

What ERC cannot check

That the circuit does the right thing. ERC does not know that VBUS must tie to the load side of the shunt, that a reverse diode must sit upstream of it, or that a 0.1 Ω shunt limits the design to 0.82 A. Those are the design review's job.

Netlist agreement

The JSON design and the KiCad project are separate descriptions of one circuit, so a script compares the netlist KiCad exports against the design file, pin by pin, as part of the test suite. 42 pins, 8 nets, no differences.

kicad-cli sch erc dcm-hw-v1.kicad_sch python3 hardware/design/compare_netlists.py
THE CORRECTION THIS REVISION MAKES

Revision A treated the sensor as a module and never modelled VBUS. On the bare INA226 that is pin 8, a separate input that must be tied to IN− for load-side measurement — the datasheet's own example does exactly that. Without it the bus reading is taken from the wrong node and every power figure is wrong while looking plausible. Revision B specifies the bare IC, wires VBUS to VLOAD, straps A0 and A1 to ground for address 0x40, and marks ALERT as a no-connect.

06 / FIRMWARE

A core that runs
on a desktop.

The arithmetic lives in a platform-free C++ core with no Arduino headers, so it can be compiled and tested without a board. The application layer that touches I²C and the serial port is separate.

Modules

  • Register decode (INA226, no I²C)
  • Calibration per channel
  • Power and trapezoidal energy
  • Missing-data policy and counters
  • I²C and serial application layer

Missing-data policy

An invalid reading costs two intervals — the one ending at it and the one starting from it. A gap longer than five seconds is not integrated at all. Both are counted, so a period with no measurement cannot look like a period of zero power.

Timing

Milliseconds since boot in an unsigned 32-bit counter; subtraction wraps correctly after 49.7 days. There is no real-time clock in this design, so no date or time of day is claimed.

What has run

Three host suites — 69 + 29 + 10 checks — and a real cross-compilation: PlatformIO 6.2.0 with the espressif32 8.0.0 platform and the Xtensa GCC 14.2.0 toolchain builds firmware.bin for esp32dev, using 25.4 % of flash and 7.2 % of RAM, with no warnings at -Wall -Wextra. It has never been flashed or run.

pio run -e esp32dev g++ -std=c++17 -I include -o /tmp/t src/core/*.cpp test/test_core.cpp

07 / OPEN QUESTIONS

What only the
physical module can answer.

Six questions that must be answered with a multimeter before the ESP32 is connected to anything.

  1. Address strapsDoes the module actually sit at 0x40? Read it with an I²C scanner.
  2. Pull-up railDo the module's pull-ups go to 3.3 V, 5 V, or the module supply? Measure before wiring.
  3. VBUS connectionIs the bus voltage taken at IN−? If the pin is separate, wire it there.
  4. On-board shuntWhat value is fitted, and does it sit in parallel with the external 0.1 Ω?
  5. Common-mode rangeDoes the module's amplifier accept a 12 V common-mode at the shunt?
  6. Supply qualityDoes the 3.3 V rail hold up under I²C traffic?
